About Padel

Discover the sport that's taking the world by storm. Learn the rules, history, and unique characteristics that make Padel so addictive.

What is Padel?

The Game

Padel is a racquet sport played in doubles on an enclosed court roughly one-third the size of a tennis court. The court is surrounded by walls of glass and metallic mesh, which can be used during play, adding a unique strategic element to the game.

The Rules

Similar to tennis, but with key differences: underarm serving, no volleys in the service box, and the ball can hit the walls after bouncing on the ground. Points are scored using the same system as tennis (15, 30, 40, game).

The History of Padel

Origins (1969)

Padel was invented in 1969 by Enrique Corcuera at his home in Acapulco, Mexico. He wanted to build a tennis court but was limited by space, so he enclosed it with walls. The game quickly gained popularity among his friends and neighbors.

Growth in Spain (1970s)

Alfonso de Hohenlohe, a Spanish friend of Corcuera, brought the sport to Spain in the mid-1970s. The first Padel court in Spain was built at the Marbella Club in 1974, and from there, the sport spread rapidly throughout the country.

Global Expansion (1990s-Present)

In the 1990s, Padel began expanding to other countries, particularly in Latin America. Argentina became a major hub for the sport, producing many top professional players. The World Padel Tour, established in 2013, has helped professionalize and globalize the sport.

Modern Era

Today, Padel is played in over 90 countries with more than 25 million active players worldwide. It's particularly popular in Spain, Argentina, Mexico, and is rapidly growing in Europe, the Middle East, and North America.

Court Layout & Basic Rules

🏟️

Court Dimensions

  • • 20m long × 10m wide (66ft × 33ft)
  • • Enclosed by 4m high walls on the ends
  • • 3m high walls on the sides
  • • Glass walls at the back, mesh on sides
  • • Net height: 0.88m at ends, 0.92m in center
⚖️

Key Rules

  • • Always played in doubles (4 players)
  • • Underarm serving only
  • • Ball must bounce before hitting walls
  • • No volleys in service boxes
  • • Same scoring system as tennis
🎯

Scoring

  • • Points: 15, 30, 40, Game
  • • Sets: Best of 3 or 5 sets
  • • Games: First to 6 games wins set
  • • Tiebreaker at 6-6
  • • Match formats vary by tournament

How to Play

Serving

Service Rules

The serve must be underarm and hit below waist level. The ball must bounce in the service box diagonally opposite and can hit the side or back wall after bouncing, but not before.

Service Positions

Serve from behind the service line, alternating between right and left sides. Players switch sides after each game, just like in tennis.

Rally Play

Wall Usage

After the ball bounces on your side, it can hit the walls on your side of the court. You can also hit the ball onto the opponent's wall after it crosses the net.

Strategy

Use the walls to create angles, play defensive shots, and set up attacking positions. Communication with your partner is crucial for effective doubles play.

What Makes Padel Special?

🤝

Social Aspect

Padel is inherently social - always played in doubles, encouraging communication, teamwork, and friendship. The smaller court keeps all players involved in every point.

🧠

Strategic Depth

The walls add layers of strategy not found in other racquet sports. Every shot can potentially use the walls, creating endless tactical possibilities and exciting rallies.

🎓

Accessibility

Easier to learn than tennis due to underarm serving and wall play that keeps balls in play longer. Players of different skill levels can enjoy the game together.

💪

Fitness Benefits

Great cardiovascular workout with constant movement, improved reflexes, hand-eye coordination, and mental agility from the fast-paced, strategic gameplay.
